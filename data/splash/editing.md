# On Reading What Machines Write

There is a particular kind of document that did not exist a few years ago: the
report an agent writes *for you*, continuously, while it works. Plans that rewrite
themselves. Notes that grow between glances. Plain Markdown, but never still.

## A page, not a panel

Most tools show that Markdown the way they show *everything else* — as chrome, in
the same flat interface as the buttons and menus around it. But reading is not the
same activity as clicking. A wall of prose deserves a **page**: a comfortable
measure, a typeface chosen for long-form text, and colours that rest the eye
rather than demand it.

That is what a *reading theme* is for. The toolbar, the tabs, the editor — those
stay on your desktop theme, crisp and utilitarian. The **preview** becomes
something you'd actually want to read:

- **Sepia** — warm off-white, a serif face, soft brown ink. A book.
- **Synthwave** — midnight indigo and neon, for reading after dark.
- **Terminal** — light grey on true black, monospaced, unmistakably a console.

## The point

> You should be able to edit on the left and *read* on the right — and have the
> right side look like it was set by someone who cares about type.

Split the window, keep writing, and watch the page keep pace. When your agent
changes the file underneath you, the words update and your place is kept. It never
touches your text; it only makes it easier to live with.
